Why a hot Mac gets slow

Every Mac has a safe temperature range. When the processor gets near the top of that range, macOS lowers its speed, because a slower processor makes less heat. Nothing breaks, but everything takes longer: apps open slowly, video stutters, and the fans get loud.

A MacBook Air has no fan, so it slows down sooner during long, heavy work, such as a video export.

What is kernel_task?

On a hot Mac, Activity Monitor often shows a process named kernel_task at the top of the CPU list. It looks like the cause, but it is part of the cure. Apple says that kernel_task helps control the processor temperature (Apple Support). It makes the processor less available to the apps that use it the most, so the processor can cool down.

When the Mac is cooler, kernel_task goes back to normal by itself. Do not try to quit it.

What makes a Mac hot

  • An app that works hard for a long time: a video export, a game, a large build, or a browser tab with a heavy web page.
  • Blocked air vents, for example a laptop on a bed, a sofa or your lap.
  • A warm room or direct sunlight.
  • A stuck process that uses the processor and never stops.

How to find the app that makes the heat

  1. Open Activity Monitor: press Command-Space, type “Activity Monitor”, and press Return.
  2. Click CPU, then click the % CPU column so that the biggest number is at the top.
  3. Skip kernel_task. The next app on the list is usually the one that makes the heat.

How to cool your Mac down

  1. Save your work, then quit the app at the top of the CPU list. If it does not respond, select it in Activity Monitor and click the stop button (✕) in the toolbar.
  2. Put the Mac on a hard, flat surface, such as a desk or a table, and keep the air vents clear.
  3. Move out of direct sunlight or into a cooler room.
  4. Wait a few minutes. The speed comes back as the temperature goes down.

When to get help

Sometimes a Mac stays hot when nothing works hard, with kernel_task at the top of the list for a long time. If that happens, contact Apple Support. A fan or a temperature sensor can have a problem.
